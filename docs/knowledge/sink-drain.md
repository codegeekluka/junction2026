## How confident / gaps

- **High confidence:** the core procedure (bucket → open → empty → clean → check seal → hand-tighten → leak test), washer orientation on slip joints, "don't overtighten plastic", "no chemicals, then open the trap", "don't mix chemicals". Several independent sources agree [1][2][12][13][14][16].
- **Bottle-trap cleaning comes from Hansgrohe's pictograms** [1]. They show a bucket, holding the body, unscrewing the bottom cup and letting it drain. They don't say anything in words about the bottom-cup O-ring. The parts list does show an O-ring (38 × 2.5 mm) on the bottom cap [1], so "check the O-ring" is an inference from the parts list.
- **Prevex P-loc cleaning is also pictograms only** [2] (bucket under the U-bend, unscrew it, rinse, refit). One small icon *seems* to cross out a pipe wrench next to allowed hand tools. That's our reading of the icon; it isn't stated in words.
- **"Bottle traps dominate in Finland": NOT verified with data.** Evidence: Finnish retailers sell pullovesilukko and erikoisvesilukko for basins [6]; Toolstation puts bottle traps in bathrooms and P-traps under all sink types [17]; one weaker source calls the bottle trap the most popular trap in Europe [20]. Finnish **kitchen** traps (Prevex P-loc/Smartloc, Comploc) are plastic U/P-type with appliance spigots [2][11]. So the working assumption is **bathroom basin = bottle trap (or a design cleaned from above), kitchen = plastic P-trap with appliance branch**.
- **Tension with the manufacturer:** Geberit advises laypeople to *leave trap removal to a professional* and use a plunger [4]. In Finnish apartment buildings, Kiinteistöliitto (2019) says sink trap cleaning belongs to the housing company [7]. **For trust, the app should say up front: "In an apartment building you can also ask your huoltoyhtiö to do this."**
- **Conflict on chemicals:** Kiinteistöliitto suggests trying a drain opener first, then calling the service company [7]. That clashes with "never open a trap after chemicals" [16]. Our rule: if any chemical went in today, do not open the trap.
- **Could not verify:** Gustavsberg, Oras, Grohe, IKEA or Viega trap cleaning texts (none found online; IKEA's LILLVIKEN care text only says mild soap, no abrasives). No cited source on **electrical safety** specific to under-sink trap work (sections 5 and 6 give common-sense guidance and are marked as such). ## 1. Trap types and how to recognise them on camera

A trap (vesilukko / hajulukko) holds a little standing water that blocks sewer smells [6][14]. Which type you have changes the procedure, so **identify the trap first**.

### 1a. Bottle trap (pullovesilukko, pullolukko). The most likely type under a bathroom basin in Finland
- **What it is:** a vertical cylinder ("bottle") under the basin waste. The water seal sits inside the bottle. A horizontal pipe leaves from the side to the wall [1][17].
- **Opens by:** unscrewing the **bottom cup / cap** (pohjakuppi). The trap can be emptied without removing the wall pipe [1][17].
- **Materials:** white/grey plastic (cheap, common in rentals) or chrome-plated brass/metal (visible pipework, designer basins) [1][17]. Hansgrohe's metal cap needs a **10 mm hex key** [1].
- **Visual description for the vision model:**
  - a vertical cylinder roughly fist-wide, directly below the drain hole, with a straight tube going sideways into the wall
  - a flat or ribbed bottom cap on the cylinder
  - no U-shaped bend visible

### 1b. U-shaped P-trap (putkivesilukko / U-lukko). Most kitchens; some basins
- **What it is:** a U-bend (the "J" part) between the vertical tailpiece from the sink and a horizontal arm to the wall. It looks like a P lying on its side [14][17].
- **Opens by:** two **slip nuts** (one on each end of the U) with **beveled cone washers** inside [13][14]. Some have a **cleanout nut** at the bottom of the U [14].
- **Materials:** white or grey polypropylene/PVC; older ones chrome-plated brass with rubber washers [13][14].
- **Finnish kitchen version:** grey plastic P-type trap (e.g. Prevex P-loc) with one or two bowls, a flexible or rigid outlet into the wall/floor, and optional **dishwasher/washing machine spigots** [2]. Comploc traps sold in Finland have 19 mm and 21 mm dishwasher connections [11].
- **Visual description:**
  - a U-shaped bend below the drain, lowest point lower than the wall outlet
  - two large ring nuts, one each side of the U
  - often a short side spigot or hose (appliance branch) on the vertical part

### 1c. S-trap and branch fittings
- **S-trap:** S-shaped seen from the side, outlet goes **down into the floor** instead of into the wall. It's uncommon, found in older homes, and can **siphon dry** [17]. Clean it like a P-trap (two slip nuts).
- **Appliance branch:** a side spigot or a hose clamped onto the trap or tailpiece [2][11]. Prevex says to cut the spigot open with a knife and fix the hose with a hose clamp [2]. **Do not disturb it during cleaning**; if it is removed, the hose clamp and seal must go back exactly as before.
- **Visual:** a grey/white corrugated hose (dishwasher/washing machine) clamped to a spigot on the trap.

### 1d. "Cleaned from above" designs (common Finnish alternatives)
- **Vieser erikoisvesilukko:** the trap is built into the waste body. To clean, you **lift the whole plug part up out of the drain** from inside the basin and rinse it under running water. No dismantling underneath [3].
- **Prevex EasyClean:** cleaned without taking the trap apart, which the maker says avoids leak risk [5].
- **Visual:** a big plug cap in the basin, plus a short straight body under the basin with **no bottle and no U**. → If seen, skip the under-sink procedure and guide "lift the plug out and rinse it".

### 1e. Plastic vs chrome: why it matters
- **Plastic:** soft threads cross-thread and strip easily; overtightening cracks them; hand-tight only [13][14][19].
- **Chrome / metal:** rubber washers; may need slip-joint pliers or a hex key [1][13]. **Old metal traps can be corroded**: if nuts won't move or crumble, replace the trap rather than force it [15][16].

## 2. Procedures

### 2.0 Before any procedure (both types)
1. **Safety question first:** "Have you poured any drain cleaner or chemical into this sink today?" If **yes → stop** (see section 5) [16].
2. **Try the easy fix first** (it may be all that's needed):
   - Pull out the **pop-up stopper** and clean the hair off it. Universal pop-ups lift straight out by hand [18]; rod-operated ones need the pivot nut behind the waste undone and the pivot rod pulled back first [18]. Don't wrench a stuck stopper; work it loose gently [18].
   - **Plunger:** remove the drain cover, **block the overflow hole** with a wet cloth and your hand so pressure builds, then pump [4][14]. Have enough water in the basin to cover the cup [12].
3. **Apartment building?** Mention that the huoltoyhtiö/housing company can do this [7].
4. **Empty the sink cabinet** so things don't get wet [6].
5. If the basin is full of standing water, **bail it out with a cup into the bucket first** [12].
6. **Bucket directly under the trap**, towel on the cabinet floor, gloves on [1][6][12][16].
7. Optional but smart: **take a photo** of the trap before taking it apart, so it goes back the same way. Keep small parts in a container [18].

### 2.1 Bottle trap (pullovesilukko)
Based on Hansgrohe [1] plus general guides [6][12].

| # | Step | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | Bucket under | Bucket directly below the bottom cup (Hansgrohe marks this with a warning icon) [1]. |
| B2 | Hold the body | With one hand **hold the bottle body / upper part still**, so turning the cup does not twist the joints above [1]. |
| B3 | Unscrew the bottom cup | Turn the cup **anticlockwise** (seen from below) by hand; chrome caps may need a 10 mm hex key [1]. Water and debris drop into the bucket [1]. |
| B4 | Empty and clean | Tip out the water and gunk; scrub the cup and inside the bottle with an old toothbrush or bottle brush; rinse with warm soapy water [6][12]. Don't use abrasive pads or acid/lye cleaners on chrome [1]. |
| B5 | Check the cup seal | The cup has an **O-ring / flat ring** (Hansgrohe: 38 × 2.5 mm) [1]. It must be clean, not cracked or flattened, and sitting in its groove. Replace a hard or damaged one [15]. |
| B6 | Clean the stopper | Lift out the pop-up and remove hair (if not done in 2.0) [18]. Vieser-type plugs lift out from above and are rinsed under the tap [3]. |
| B7 | Refit the cup | Start the thread **by hand, straight**. Turning it backwards first until you feel/hear a click helps align the threads [19]. Then **hand-tight**; do not use pliers on plastic [13][14]. |
| B8 | Refill and leak test | Run water: the trap refills its seal [12]. Then fill the basin a bit and let it drain, wipe the cup joint with **dry tissue/paper**; it must stay dry [14]. |
| B9 | Done | Drains freely, no drips after a couple of minutes, bucket stays below for a while. |

### 2.2 P-trap (U-trap with slip nuts)
Based on Prevex P-loc pictograms [2], Family Handyman [13][14], Dummies [15], Which? [12].

| # | Step | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Bucket under | Bucket directly under the U-bend [2][15]. If the trap has a **cleanout nut** at the bottom, open that first and let it drain [14]. |
| P2 | Loosen slip nuts | Unscrew the **two slip nuts** at each end of the U, by hand if possible, slip-joint pliers only if stuck (metal) [12][15]. Support the U with your other hand. |
| P3 | Remove the U | Lower the U-bend into the bucket, keeping it level until it's over the bucket. It's full of dirty water [2][16]. |
| P4 | Note the washers | Each joint has a **beveled cone washer**. Look at which way it faces before it falls off [13][14]. |
| P5 | Empty and clean | Empty into the bucket, scrub inside with a bottle brush or straightened hanger, rinse under another tap [2][15]. Wipe the pipe ends and washer seats clean, because debris between the washer and the pipe causes leaks [15]. |
| P6 | Check the drain beyond | If the U was clean, the clog is further on (wall pipe). A small hand snake can be fed into the wall pipe [15], **but in an apartment building, prefer the huoltoyhtiö** (section 5) [8]. |
| P7 | Check washers | Washers should be **soft and supple**; replace stiff, cracked or deformed ones [13][15]. Orientation: **flat side against the nut, tapered side towards the fitting/trap** [13][14]. |
| P8 | Reassemble | Slide nut then washer onto the pipe, push the U in **straight**. A crooked joint leaks even with new washers [13]. Start nuts **by hand** [13]. Washers must not be twisted [15]. |
| P9 | Tighten | **Hand-tight, then at most ¼ turn with pliers** (Family Handyman: "finger-tight plus a 1/4 turn"); some guides allow ½ turn on metal [13][14]. **No tape or putty** on slip joints [14]. |
| P10 | Leak test | Run water, then drain a few centimetres of water from the full basin; dab every joint with **dry tissue** [14]. If one drips, tighten that nut a little; if it still drips, reopen and check washer direction and alignment [15]. |
| P11 | Done | Drains freely, all joints dry. Appliance hose (if any) still clamped [2]. |

## 3. What "done correctly" vs "done wrong" looks like (visual cues)

> Our wording, built from sections 1–2. Every cue is about the **foreground** (the trap and hands), so it's checkable on one frame.

| Step | Looks correct | Looks wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Prep: cabinet | Cabinet floor visible, towel down | Bottles and cleaners still crowded around the trap |
| Prep: bucket | Bucket opening **directly below** the bottom cup (bottle) or U-bend (P-trap); towel under it | No bucket; bucket off to one side; small cup instead of a bucket |
| Prep: gloves | Gloved hands | Bare hands while handling waste water (soft warning) |
| Stopper | Pop-up stopper out of the drain, hair visible on it or removed | Stopper still in, or being yanked hard |
| Bottle: hold | One hand on the bottle body, the other on the cup | Only one hand twisting the cup; joints above visibly turning |
| Bottle: cup off | Cup removed, **gap visible** under the bottle body, water falling into the bucket | Cup half off and dripping beside the bucket |
| P: nuts | Hand on a slip nut; U supported by the other hand | Pliers/pipe wrench on a **plastic** nut; U hanging from one nut |
| P: U off | U-bend held level over/in the bucket, both pipe ends open | U-bend tipped sideways, dirty water spilling on the cabinet |
| Clean | Inside of cup/U visibly free of hair and sludge; brush or toothbrush in use | Gunk still visible in the cup/bend |
| Seal (bottle) | O-ring **seated flat, all the way round, in the groove** of the cup | Ring missing, twisted, hanging out of the groove, or lying in the bucket |
| Washer (P) | Cone washer on the pipe, **tapered side towards the trap**, flat side towards the nut | Washer missing; washer reversed; washer twisted or skewed |
| Alignment | Pipe enters the nut straight, nut sits square | Nut visibly tilted; pipes at an angle at the joint |
| Thread start | Cup/nut turning easily by hand for several turns | Nut stuck after less than a turn, or at an angle (cross-threading) |
| Tighten | Hand on nut, no tool (plastic) | Pliers or wrench on plastic, knuckles white, nut deforming |
| Appliance hose | Hose still clamped on the spigot | Hose loose or off the spigot |
| Leak test | Water running, dry tissue against the joint, tissue dry | Drops forming under a joint; wet tissue; water in the bucket increasing |

## 4. Common beginner mistakes (each → a spoken warning)

| # | Mistake | What goes wrong | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Opening the trap after using drain cleaner** | Caustic liquid sits above the clog and pours out onto hands/face → chemical burns | [16] |
| M2 | **No bucket / bucket not under the trap** | The trap is full of dirty water; it ends up in the cabinet and on the floor | [1][2][6][12] |
| M3 | **Overtightening plastic** (especially with pliers) | Cracked nut or fitting, distorted washer → leak, water damage | [13][14] |
| M4 | **Pliers / pipe wrench on plastic** | Teeth gouge and crack the plastic; point pressure cracks fittings | [14]; Prevex pictogram (our reading) [2] |
| M5 | **Lost, reversed or twisted washer / O-ring** | Joint leaks even when tight | [13][14][15] |
| M6 | **Cross-threading** (starting the nut at an angle, then forcing it) | Plastic threads ruined → nut or trap must be replaced | [19][13] |
| M7 | **Misaligned joint** | Leaks even with new washers | [13] |
| M8 | **Forgetting the pop-up stopper** | Hair on the stopper is often the real clog; the sink stays slow | [18] |
| M9 | **Plunging without blocking the overflow** | Pressure escapes through the overflow; plunging does nothing | [4][14] |
| M10 | **Mixing chemicals** (drain cleaner + bleach / acid / vinegar etc.) | Toxic gas (chlorine or chloramine) | [12][16] |
| M11 | **Tape, putty or silicone on slip joints** | Not how slip joints seal; the washer does it | [14] |
| M12 | **Pushing wires or sticks into a blocked main drain** | Makes the blockage tighter and harder to clear | [8] |
| M13 | **Forcing a seized or corroded metal trap** | Breaks the trap or the wall connection | [15][16] |
| M14 | **Twisting the cup without holding the bottle body** | The joints above loosen → new leak higher up (our inference from Hansgrohe's "hold here" pictogram) | [1] |
| M15 | **Not refilling the trap / no leak test** | Sewer smell, or a slow drip found days later | [12][14] |

## 5. Safety, and when to stop and call a professional

### Hard stops (the coach stops the procedure)
- **Chemical drain cleaner used today** → do **not** open the trap, plunge or snake. Splash-back burns. Tell the plumber/huoltoyhtiö what product, how much and when [16].
  - Drain openers sold in Finland are typically strongly alkaline (sodium hydroxide), classed H314/H318: **severe skin burns and eye damage** [21].
  - **Splash in the eye:** rinse with water **at least 30 minutes** for corrosives; on skin, remove clothing and rinse 30–60 min. **Myrkytystietokeskus 0800 147 111** (24/7); emergency **112** [10].
- **Water keeps rising / several drains slow / clog not in the trap** → the blockage is further down the line. If all drains are slow, it's likely the main line → professional [15]. In Finnish apartment buildings: **call the huoltoyhtiö**; they can bring in a pipe company or suction truck [8]. Don't push anything in [8].
  - Why it matters: one blocked building drain backed sewage into a ground-floor flat, >€18,000 damage; the person who caused it can be liable [9].
- **Cracked, corroded or crumbling parts; nuts that won't turn** → replace the trap or call a pro; don't force it [15][16]. Hansgrohe: replace damaged components, risk of injury [1].
- **Leak from the wall connection or behind the wall** → not a trap job. Call the huoltoyhtiö / plumber. (Our rule; consistent with Kiinteistöliitto's point that wrongly reassembled traps leak and cause water damage [7].)
- **Still leaking after one re-seat and re-tighten** → stop, put the bucket back, call a pro [7][13].
- **Electricity nearby** (sockets, dishwasher plug, water heater in the sink cabinet): keep water away. If water is on or near a socket, don't touch it; switch off at the fuse board. *(No specific cited source found; general guidance. Appliance installers say to cut power before disconnecting under-sink appliances.)*

### Soft warnings
- Gloves: waste water is dirty; chemical-resistant gloves and eye protection if any chemical may be present [16].
- In apartment buildings, sink trap cleaning is the **housing company's** job under Kiinteistöliitto's 2019 interpretation, *because* wrongly reassembled traps leak [7]. The user may do it, but should know they can ask.

## 6. Tools and parts

**Essential**
- Bucket (big enough to sit under the trap) [1][6][12]
- Old towels / rags [12][16]
- Rubber gloves [12]
- Old toothbrush and/or bottle brush [6][12][15]
- Warm water + washing-up liquid [6][12]

**Sometimes**
- Plunger (block the overflow) [4][14]
- Small hand drain snake (only for the wall pipe, only if you own the building or the huoltoyhtiö agrees) [15]
- Slip-joint pliers (**metal nuts only**, or ¼ turn max) [13][14]
- 10 mm hex key (Hansgrohe chrome bottle trap cap) [1]
- Torch / phone light

**Spare parts worth having**
- Spare **cone washers / O-rings** in the right size (e.g. 32 mm basin, 40 mm kitchen); seal kits (tiivistesarja) are sold in Finnish hardware stores [6][13]
- Replacement trap if the old one is cracked or corroded [15]

**Not needed / don't use**
- Chemical drain cleaner before opening a trap [16]
- Tape, putty or silicone on slip joints [14]
- Abrasive pads, acid or lye cleaners on chrome [1]

## 7. Suggested checks for the vision checker

> Questions are yes/no about the **foreground only** (trap, hands, bucket). Ask a **gate** question first (is the trap/sink in view?), then the step question, then mistake checks. All wording is ours.

### 7.0 Shared (every step)
- **Gate:** "Is a sink drain trap (pipework directly under a sink) clearly visible in the foreground?"
- **Type ID (once, multiple choice):** "Which trap is under this sink: (a) a vertical cylinder with a screw-off bottom cap (bottle trap), (b) a U-shaped bend with a nut on each side (P-trap), (c) a short straight body with no bottle or bend, (d) can't tell?"
- **Safety (spoken, not visual):** "Any drain cleaner today?" → yes = stop.

### 7.1 Bottle trap

| Step | Yes/no questions (done) | Mistakes to watch (question → warning) |
|---|---|---|
| B0 Stopper | "Is the sink's pop-up stopper lifted out of the drain hole?" | "Is a person forcing or levering the stopper with a tool?" → "Gently, wiggle it out" |
| B1 Bucket | "Is there a bucket on the floor directly below the bottom of the bottle-shaped trap?" | "Is the bucket off to one side so the trap is not above it?" → "Move the bucket right under the cap" |
| B2 Hold | "Is one hand holding the cylindrical body of the trap while the other holds its bottom cap?" | "Is the trap being turned with only one hand on the bottom cap?" → "Hold the body with your other hand" |
| B3 Cup off | "Is the bottom cap removed so there is an open gap under the trap body?" · "Is water or debris falling into the bucket?" | "Is water dripping outside the bucket?" → "Stop, move the bucket" |
| B4 Clean | "Is the inside of the removed cap free of hair and dark sludge?" | "Is a metal scourer or abrasive pad being used on a chrome part?" → "Use a toothbrush instead" |
| B5 Seal | "Is a rubber ring visible sitting flat all the way round in the groove of the cap?" | "Is the rubber ring missing, twisted, or sticking out of the groove?" → "Seat the ring flat before you screw it on" |
| B7 Refit | "Is the bottom cap screwed back onto the trap body, sitting straight and level?" | "Is the cap visibly tilted on the body?" → "Unscrew and start it straight" · "Are pliers or a wrench on the plastic cap?" → "Hand-tight only" |
| B8 Leak test | "Is water running from the tap into the sink?" · "Is the area under the trap cap dry, with no drops forming?" | "Is a water drop hanging from or falling off the trap?" → "Leak: hand-tighten a little; if it still drips, check the ring" |

### 7.2 P-trap

| Step | Yes/no questions (done) | Mistakes to watch (question → warning) |
|---|---|---|
| P0 Stopper / overflow | "Is the pop-up stopper removed from the drain?" · (if plunging) "Is the overflow hole covered by a hand or cloth?" | "Is someone plunging with the overflow hole uncovered?" → "Block the overflow first" |
| P1 Bucket | "Is a bucket directly below the U-shaped bend?" | "Is there no bucket under the trap?" → "No bucket, stop before you loosen anything" |
| P2 Nuts | "Is a hand turning one of the large ring nuts on the trap?" | "Is a pipe wrench or pliers gripping a plastic nut?" → "Use your hand, pliers crack plastic" |
| P3 U off | "Is the U-shaped bend detached and held level over the bucket?" | "Is dirty water spilling from the U-bend outside the bucket?" → "Keep it level over the bucket" |
| P5 Clean | "Is the inside of the U-bend free of visible hair and sludge?" | — |
| P7 Washer | "Is a cone-shaped washer on the pipe end with its narrow, tapered side facing the trap and its flat side facing the nut?" | "Is the washer missing, reversed (flat side towards the trap) or twisted?" → "Flip it: tapered side towards the trap" |
| P8 Reassemble | "Are both ends of the U-bend pushed straight into their nuts, with no visible angle at the joints?" | "Is a nut visibly tilted on its thread?" → "Cross-threaded: back it off and restart by hand" · "Is the appliance hose disconnected from the spigot?" → "Reconnect and clamp the hose" |
| P9 Tighten | "Is the nut being turned by hand without a tool?" | "Is a tool being used with big force on a plastic nut?" → "Hand-tight plus a quarter turn, no more" |
| P10 Leak test | "Is water running into the sink?" · "Are both slip-nut joints dry, with no drops forming?" | "Is a water drop forming at either joint?" → "Leak: tighten that nut slightly; if still dripping, check the washer and alignment" |

### 7.3 Escalation triggers (any step)
- "Is the water level in the sink rising while the tap is off?" → stop, call huoltoyhtiö / plumber [8][15].
- "Is there visible rust, green corrosion, or a crack on the trap or its nuts?" → stop, replace or call a pro [15][16].
- "Is water leaking where the pipe enters the wall or floor?" → stop, call a pro.
- "Is an electrical socket or plug wet or close to running water?" → stop, power off at the fuse board.
